# Sales playbook

Summary: the operating reference for everything Sonagi does to win and convert a customer. It consolidates four canonical sources so a session never loads raw books at runtime. It governs the COMMERCIAL surfaces (the store, product pages, paid ads, lead magnets, launches, promo emails, bundles, pricing). It does not govern the editorial Reference encyclopedia voice, which has its own rules. When to touch: only Asma changes the strategy or the honesty line. Everyone else applies the frameworks.

The one rule above all the tactics: **Sonagi persuades with the truth, harder.** Every device below works only when the underlying claim is real. A fake countdown, an invented review, a manufactured shortage, or a fear with no genuine fix is both a brand risk and, per Cialdini, the exact pattern customers are trained to punish. We do not use any of them.

## Part 1. The Offer (Hormozi, $100M Offers)

The offer is the thing you sell plus the terms. Get it right and copy barely matters. Get it wrong and no copy saves it. Order of leverage: **a great market beats a great offer beats great persuasion.**

### The Value Equation (the core lens)
```
Value = (Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        ----------------------------------------------------------
        (Time Delay  ×  Effort & Sacrifice)
```
Increase the top two, decrease the bottom two. Every offer and every line of copy should move at least one lever.
- **Dream Outcome.** What they truly want underneath the product. Nearly always ladders up to status (to be seen better, to not lose face). For Sonagi: not "a serum" but glass-skin, the bare-faced confidence, the compliment.
- **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement.** Their belief it will work for them. Raised by proof: before/after, real reviews, dermatologist or formulator credibility, a guarantee.
- **Time Delay.** How fast they see a result. "Fast beats free." Always pair a fast first win (first-use sensorial payoff, "softer by morning") with the long outcome ("brighter in 8 weeks").
- **Effort & Sacrifice.** What it costs them in work, steps, discomfort. Fewer steps, a simple routine, done-for-you regimen bundles. The supplement market dwarfs the gym market because it asks less effort. Make it immediate, seamless, effortless.

### Market selection (choose before you build)
Four indicators of a market worth serving: **massive pain** ("the pain is the pitch"), **purchasing power**, **easy to target**, **growing**. The three durable markets are health, wealth, relationships. Skincare sits on health plus status/relationships, which is why it prices well. The more niched the product, the rarer it reads, the more you can charge.

### The Grand Slam Offer
Hormozi's definition: an offer "you cannot compare to any other product or service available, combining an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price, and an unbeatable guarantee," with a money model that lets you get paid to acquire customers. The goal: "make people an offer so good they feel stupid saying no."

### Build it in 5 steps
1. **Identify the dream outcome.** The real end state, not the product.
2. **List every problem** between them and that outcome, before AND after purchase. Write all of them. Each problem is a future bonus or a future objection to answer.
3. **Turn each problem into a named solution.** "Buying clean skincare is confusing" becomes "the 3-step routine cheat sheet."
4. **Choose delivery vehicles (the Delivery Cube / cheat codes).** For each solution decide: attention level (1-to-1, small group, 1-to-many), client effort (do-it-themselves, do-it-with, done-for-you), live method, recorded format, response speed. Then run the **10x / one-tenth test**: what would you add if you charged 10x, what would you strip at one-tenth, and deliver accordingly.
5. **Trim and stack.** The **Sales-to-Fulfillment Continuum**: "when it's easy to sell, it's hard to fulfill, and vice versa." Cut high-cost low-value items, keep high-value items (especially low-cost-to-deliver, infinitely scalable ones), then bundle the survivors into one integrated offer.

### Pricing
- Charge a **premium**. "If you can't be 10 times cheaper than your competitors, don't compete on price." Inflate perceived value instead.
- "**Those who pay the most pay the most attention.**" Higher price lifts perceived value, emotional investment, and actual results, and attracts better customers.
- Think **divergent**, not convergent: there are many possible offers, find the one that is more right, do not romanticize how you currently solve it.
- "Deliver as much value as you can for as high a price as you can."

### The 5 enhancers (apply after the core offer is strong)
**Scarcity** (limit supply). Three honest types: limited total supply, limited bonus supply, never-available-again. For products: real limited drops/batches. Demand is exponential, not linear: a slice of buyers will pay several times the base price, so fewer units at higher prices can earn more and read as more exclusive. Keep supply slightly below demand. **Only when the limit is real.**

**Urgency** (set a deadline). "Deadlines drive decisions." Four honest types: cohort/rolling ("order by X for this drop"), rolling seasonal (a genuine monthly promo you honor), price-or-bonus-based ("the gift-with-purchase ends Sunday"), exploding opportunity (a real one-time launch window). Honor every deadline you state or lose credibility.

**Bonuses** (stack value). "A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into its component parts and stacked as bonuses." Bonuses beat discounts: a discount teaches the price is soft, a bonus preserves it. Each bonus should kill one specific objection, and the stacked bonus value should exceed the core price. The **bonus-bullet formula** for presenting each one: benefit-driven name, the problem it solves, what it is, how you made it (effort), the better life with it, proof, a justified value tag, and real scarcity/urgency if true.

**Guarantees** (reverse the risk). Formula: "**If you don't get X within Y, we will Z.**" Neutralizes the buyer's core fear that it will not work for them. Four types, weakest to strongest leverage of trust: **unconditional** (no-questions refund, strongest signal), **conditional** (refund tied to them doing the steps, which also drives results), **anti-guarantee** (all sales final, used when the product is rare/premium), **implied** (you are paid only on their result). Name the guarantee. People rarely abuse it, and a longer/stronger guarantee usually lifts sales more than it costs in refunds.

**Naming (MAGIC).** Refresh a name when the offer hits fatigue (the offer stays, the wrapper changes). Build the name from a few of these, not all: **M**agnet (an attractive reason/theme), **A**vatar (who it is for), **G**oal (the outcome), **I**nterval (the timeframe), **C**ontainer (a system word: ritual, cure, blueprint, edit, drop). Shorter and more specific wins.

## Part 2. Getting leads (Hormozi, $100M Leads)

A **lead** is anyone you can contact. An **engaged lead** shows interest. "Engaged leads are the true output of advertising." Everything here exists to manufacture engaged leads.

### The Lead Magnet
Definition: "a complete solution to a narrow problem" you give free or cheap, that reveals the next problem your core product solves. "A person who pays with their time now is more likely to pay with their money later." Rule: **"Give away the secrets, sell the implementation."**
- **7 steps:** pick the problem and who for, solve it, choose how to deliver it, test the name, make it easy to consume, make it genuinely great, make it easy to say "I want more."
- **3 types:** reveal-the-problem (a diagnosis, e.g. a free skin/routine quiz), sample or trial (Hormozi names cosmetics explicitly: the sachet/mini is the textbook beauty lead magnet), one step of a multi-step process.
- For Sonagi: a **skin quiz** (diagnosis) and a **deluxe mini or sample** (trial) are the two best fits.

### The Core Four (the only four ways to get known)
Two audiences (warm = gave you permission, cold = strangers) by two modes (1-to-1, 1-to-many):
- Warm + 1-to-1 = **Warm Outreach**
- Warm + 1-to-many = **Post Content**
- Cold + 1-to-1 = **Cold Outreach**
- Cold + 1-to-many = **Paid Ads**

For a 2-person pre-launch DTC brand, the realistic set is **Post Content + Warm Outreach + Referrals + creator-affiliates + UGC paid ads.** Cold outreach (built around scraped B2B lists), employees, and agencies do not transfer to consumer skincare now. Skip them.

### Post Content (the primary engine when you have more time than money)
The content unit is **Hook, Retain, Reward.** Hook = topic + headline + format (steal the platform's best format). Retain = curiosity via lists, steps, stories. Reward = value per second ("there's no such thing as too long, only too boring"). Switch "How to" to "How I." Monetize with **"give, give, give, until they ask"** (give in public, ask in private); the moment you start asking is the moment growth slows, so over-give while young.

### Volume and consistency
**The Rule of 100:** 100 primary actions a day for 100 days (100 outreaches, or 100 minutes making content plus a daily post, or 100 minutes making ads). **Better** comes only from testing: find the biggest drop-off step (the constraint) and test one thing there per week.

### Referrals (the highest-leverage, lowest-cost channel)
"The best advertising is a happy customer." **Referral growth = referrals in minus churn out.** Two reasons brands do not get them: the product is not good enough, or they never ask. **Goodwill = value minus price**; build it by over-delivering, not discounting. Seven ways to ask, the strongest for DTC being a **two-sided incentive** (give-and-get, the Dropbox/PayPal pattern), asking **at the moment of value**, and **named promotions** ("bring a friend"). Creator-affiliates are referrals at scale: find creators whose audience is full of your customer, give them your sample as a bonus inside their own offer.

## Part 3. The copy (Whitman, Cashvertising)

"People buy on emotion and justify with logic." Write to the desire, then give the logic to defend it.

### What people actually want
**The Life-Force 8 (biological, the strongest levers):** survival/life-extension; food and drink; freedom from fear, pain, danger; sexual companionship; comfortable living; to be superior / keep up; care of loved ones; **social approval.** "Biology is king." For skincare the live wires are **social approval + to be superior** (the glow, the compliment), and **freedom from fear** (aging, sun damage, breakouts, sensitivity) when there is a real fix. The **9 learned wants** (beauty/style, cleanliness, convenience, quality, bargains, etc.) are weaker, so anchor "beauty" back to an LF8 root (approval, attraction) rather than selling beauty as an end.

### The psychology that maps to skincare
- **Means-End Chain (sell the benefit of the benefit).** Not "niacinamide serum," but "the bare-skin confidence you walk out with."
- **Ego morphing / aspiration.** Show the image they want to merge with; feel-good beauty needs little argument.
- **Bandwagon + social proof.** "The one French girls keep rebuying." Real popularity only.
- **Transfer / authority.** Borrow credibility honestly: dermatologist-tested, formulator story, lab provenance.
- **Fear, used ethically.** Only an existing, specific, widely-felt fear, and only paired with a recommended action the reader believes they can take and that genuinely works.
- **Elaboration Likelihood.** Cheap impulse buy persuades by mood and imagery (peripheral); a hero serum or device persuades by ingredients and proof (central). Match the route to the price.

### The copy mechanics
- **Simplicity.** Short words (most one syllable), short sentences (about 11 words), short paragraphs, pile on "you" and "I." "Write to the chimpanzee brain. Simply. Directly."
- **Benefits over features.** After every feature ask "so what?" until you hit the human payoff.
- **Biggest benefit in the headline.** "Unless your headline sells your product, you have wasted 90 percent of your money." About 60% read only the headline. Pull from the **22 headline starters** (At Last, New, Warning, How, Which of, Do You, If You, Announcing, Finally, etc.). Caples' four qualities: self-interest, news, curiosity, quick-and-easy way. Always answer **WIIFM** (what's in it for me).
- **PVAs and mental movies.** Powerful Visual Adjectives plus the five senses (sight, sound, touch, smell, taste). Describe the texture, the slip, the scent, the finish: "a cushiony gel that melts into a dewy veil." "The first use of any product is inside the customer's mind."
- **Extreme specificity.** Specifics out-sell generalities and read as proof. "10,000 ppm galactomyces, cold-processed within 48 hours of harvest" beats "natural ingredients."
- **The face (Guillotine principle).** A human face looking at the reader is the strongest attention-getter and adds trust. Founder face, relatable model, product-in-use beats product-alone.
- **Proof and long copy.** "Copy can never be too long, only too boring." Full product pages with deep ingredient and benefit detail out-sell thin ones. Stack real testimonials.
- **Guarantee, white space, pricing.** Offer the longest honest guarantee in the category. Use generous white space for a premium read. Pricing psychology: round whole numbers signal prestige (luxury serum), .99/.97 endings signal value (entry items). Frame cost-per-use ("less than the price of a coffee a week").
- **Cleverectomy.** "In advertising, it's not clever to be clever." Effective over cute. The headline must say what you sell.

The **AIDA** spine for any asset: get **A**ttention, build **I**nterest, create **D**esire, drive **A**ction. End every asset by asking for the action.

## Part 4. The persuasion principles (Cialdini, Influence)

The seven principles (this is the expanded 7-principle edition). For each: the honest Sonagi use, then the line we do not cross.
1. **Reciprocity.** Give first (a real sample, a genuinely useful skin guide), and a felt goodwill follows. **Line:** no strings-attached "pseudo-gift" engineered to extract an outsized return.
2. **Liking.** Similarity, sincere praise, familiarity, and association persuade. Build a relatable founder and a warm, repeated presence. **Line:** real warmth, not flattery as a lever.
3. **Social proof.** "Pointing to a thing's popularity makes it more popular." Use real review counts, real UGC, "most-loved" labels. **Line:** never fake proof, and never normalize the problem ("everyone has bad skin"), which backfires.
4. **Authority.** Expertise plus trustworthiness persuades most. Lead with real credentials, and openly name one small limitation before the strengths (it builds trust and is honest). **Line:** no borrowed authority you have not earned.
5. **Scarcity.** "We value things more when fewer are available," driven by loss aversion. Real limited batches and restock dates. **Line:** scarcity raises arousal that clouds judgment, so faking it is the cardinal sin. True only.
6. **Commitment and consistency.** Small honest micro-commitments (a quiz, a skin-goal opt-in) make the next step feel consistent. **Line:** no low-ball or bait-and-switch pricing.
7. **Unity (the "we").** A shared identity ("your skin type, your ritual, our kind of person") persuades because it is in-group. **Line:** the community must be real, not a manufactured tribe.

Cialdini's own defense test is our authoring test: if a tactic only works because the customer cannot see it clearly, do not use it.

## Part 5. Sonagi honesty guardrails (the hard lines)
These bind every asset built from this skill.
1. **Scarcity and urgency must be literally true.** Real batch limits, real restock and deadline dates. No evergreen "ends tonight," no fake stock counters, no countdowns that reset.
2. **Proof must be real.** No invented reviews, ratings, or before/afters. No fabricated "number one" claims.
3. **Fear only with a real fix, framed positively.** Lead with the gain, not the shame. Never blame the reader. (Sonagi positive-framing rule.)
4. **Never sexualize.** Desire and attraction read through faces, glow, and confidence, never through bare/clinging/wet-cling framing. (Sonagi rule, same severity as the em-dash ban.)
5. **No invented product details.** Texture, scent, finish, behavior come from a real demo, never from imagination.
6. **Define jargon on first use.** Every active, ingredient, or technical term gets a plain gloss.
7. **No em dashes in published copy.** Periods, commas, colons, parentheses. (Internal skill/strategy files are exempt; published copy is not.)
8. **Surface boundary.** This playbook is for the commercial store, ads, lead magnets, launches, and emails. The editorial Reference encyclopedia keeps its own voice and its friend-not-pricetag product blocks. Do not import retail-offer pressure into editorial articles.

## Part 6. Quick-reference workflows

**Writing a product page (PDP).** Dream outcome in the headline (benefit of the benefit) → first fast win + the long outcome (Time Delay) → PVAs and a sensory mental movie of first use → extreme-specificity ingredient proof → real social proof and a face → honest guarantee → premium round-number price with cost-per-use framing → one clear CTA. Run the Value Equation over the whole page: which lever does each block move.

**Designing an offer or bundle.** Dream outcome → list every problem → name a solution for each → pick delivery vehicles (10x/one-tenth test) → trim and stack into one integrated offer → add only the enhancers that are true (real scarcity, honored deadline, stacked bonuses each killing one objection, a named guarantee) → name it with a few MAGIC parts.

**Building a lead magnet.** Pick one narrow problem your product's first step solves → choose type (skin quiz = diagnosis, mini/sample = trial) → make it genuinely worth paying for → "give away the secrets, sell the implementation" → easy to consume → a CTA that makes wanting more easy.

**Planning a launch or promo.** Pick the true urgency type (drop window, honored seasonal promo, ending gift-with-purchase) → stack real bonuses → set the honest guarantee → MAGIC name → seed with warm outreach and creator-affiliates → give-give-give content into the window → ask in private → honor every stated deadline.

**Writing an ad.** One LF8 desire → biggest-benefit headline from the 22 starters answering WIIFM → call out the avatar → AIDA body with PVAs, specificity, and one face → real proof → honest scarcity/urgency → SPELL-it-out CTA. Cleverectomy before shipping.

**Critiquing existing copy.** Score it on the Value Equation (does it raise dream and likelihood, lower time and effort), check it leads with a benefit not a feature, check the headline carries the biggest benefit, check proof is real and specific, check WIIFM is answered in the first lines, check every persuasion device is true, then run the honesty guardrails as a pass/fail gate.
